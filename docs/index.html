Getting Chore Coin running

It's a small app that needs a small computer at home to run on. Here's what that looks like in plain language.

Step 1

Buy Chore Coin

$20, one time. You'll get a license key in your email a few seconds later. Save that email — you'll need the key next.

Step 2

Pick a computer

Any computer at your house that stays on. A spare laptop works. A Mac mini works. Or grab a $15 Raspberry Pi Zero 2W — it's the size of a pack of gum and uses less power than a nightlight.

Step 3

Download & double-click

Pick the launcher for your computer below, download it, paste your license key when it asks, and wait about 30 seconds. When it's done it opens Chore Coin in your browser to finish setup.

💻 For the tech-savvy: exact install command

No download-and-double-click, just paste-and-go. All three commands do exactly the same thing as the launchers above.

macOS / Linux / Raspberry Pi — in Terminal:
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danielhemp/chore-coin/main/install.sh | \
  CHORECOIN_LICENSE=CHRC-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX sh
Windows — in PowerShell:
$env:CHORECOIN_LICENSE = 'CHRC-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX'
iwr -UseBasicParsing https://raw.githubusercontent.com/danielhemp/chore-coin/main/install.ps1 | iex
View source on GitHub →

Common questions from parents

I'm not technical. Can I really do this?

Yes. Every license comes with a step-by-step visual install guide that walks a total beginner through opening Terminal (on Mac or Linux), pasting one command, and finishing setup in the browser — with screenshots at every step. Most parents get it running in under 15 minutes on the first try. Once installed, everything is in the app on your phone; no terminal ever again. If you don't want to do it yourself, ask a tech-friendly friend for 15 minutes, or consider our paid install-help add-on ($50, done via screen-share — coming at launch).

What "computer at home" do I need?

Anything that stays on and runs Mac, Windows 10/11, or Linux. A spare laptop, a Mac mini, an old iMac someone gave you, a Windows desktop, or a $15 Raspberry Pi Zero 2W (a tiny credit-card-sized computer that plugs into a wall outlet). Chore Coin uses less RAM than a browser tab and works on hardware from the last decade.

Will it work on my kid's iPhone?

Yes. Chore Coin runs in Safari or Chrome — no App Store download needed. Open it once, then "Add to Home Screen" and it looks and feels like any other app on the phone. Works the same on Android and iPad.

Does it work in iOS Assistive Access?

Yes. If your youngest has their first phone locked down to just a few apps, Chore Coin is one of the apps you can add. Perfect for starting kids with structured phone use.

Where does my family's data live?

On the computer at your house that you installed it on. Nothing goes to us. Nothing goes to a cloud. If your internet is down, the app still works on your home Wi-Fi.

Can the family use it from Grandma's house?

Two ways. Free: set up a Cloudflare Tunnel (a free service that gives your home a public web address without opening any router ports — takes about 10 minutes). Paid: add the $12/year subdomain add-on and you'll get a nice memorable address like smiths.chore-coin.family. Either way, your data still lives at home; only the address forwards.

What if I want to switch computers later?

Two clicks. On the old computer: Settings → Download family backup. On the new computer: install Chore Coin, and during setup pick "Restore from backup." Kids, chores, coins, full history — all comes across. Then release the license on the old machine and activate it on the new one.

Is $20 really the whole price?

Yes. $20 buys the app forever, on any computer, for any number of kids and chores. Every future update is free — you'll get an in-app notice when a new version is out. The only optional cost is the $12/year subdomain add-on if you want a memorable web address, which you can skip.

What happens if you go out of business?

Nothing to you. The app keeps running on your home computer with no dependency on our servers. Your license stays valid, your data stays yours, and the source code is public — anyone with the skills can keep it working. This is the whole point of running from home.
